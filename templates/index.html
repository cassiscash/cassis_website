{# ======================== WHY CASSIS / LAYERS ========================= #}

Why Cassis

Let a hundred Bitcoin layers bloom

Bitcoin has many layers today — Lightning, Liquid, Cashu, Fedimint, Arkade, Rootstock — and new ones keep arriving. Each layer has its own tradeoffs, benefits and advantages, and it is of immense benefit to Bitcoin when people can simply pick the layer they prefer.

Until now, every new layer faced the tall order of bootstrapping its own intra-layer payment ecosystem from scratch. By making inter-layer payments seamless, Cassis encourages the creation of new layers with novel tradeoffs that wouldn’t have been worth investing in before — such as new Cashu mint designs, protocols that aren’t fully trustless but beat costly trustless setups or fragile custodial wallets, or new experiments in privacy that rethink custodianship.

Wallets stay single-layer

Wallets don’t have to implement multiple layer protocols. They stay focused on their single layer and integrate Cassis right into their payment flows as a thin layer on top of their normal operation.

No ecosystem bootstrapping tax

Any layer that can lock a payment to a hash and timeout immediately plugs into every user and merchant on every other layer.

cassis — payment flow overview

Atomic by default: every hop along a route is a simple hash time-locked contract (HTLC) locked to the receiver’s payment hash. When the receiver reveals the secret to claim the final hop, every upstream hop settles — or if anything stalls, expires and refunds.

{# ===================== MERCHANTS, OFFLINE & COP ===================== #}

Beyond simple transfers

Built for merchants, creators and real commerce

Noncustodial offline receiving

Layers like Cashu, Arkade, Fedimint, Rootstock and Liquid support asynchronous HTLC creation — locking funds to a recipient without requiring them to be online at that moment.

With a few adjustments, a payee can stay completely offline while a gateway receives any number of payments on their behalf noncustodially. Merchants and online creators can accept payments 24/7 without keeping a hot signing wallet exposed on a server.

More on offline payments →

Cassis Order Protocol (COP)

The Cassis Order Protocol provides a totally new way to buy things, subscribe to services, calculate fiat prices, place orders from a menu, and handle any other commercial interaction without leaving your wallet.

COP is almost infinitely flexible without compromising interoperability, security or privacy — and without making use of web technologies, browsers or third-party checkout pages.

{# ============================ EXAMPLES ============================= #}

Routes across the bitcoin stack

Any source, any destination

Currently Cassis has implementations for Arkade, Liquid, Fedimint, Rootstock, Cashu and Lightning, and new layers can be added easily as long as they support HTLCs.

Arkade to Cashu

Arkade → Liquid → Cashu

An Arkade user pays a Cashu user. Even if no router bridges that specific Cashu mint to Arkade directly, the payment routes seamlessly through a Liquid intermediary.

Rootstock to Lightning

Rootstock → Fedimint → Lightning

A router bridges Rootstock and a Fedimint federation, letting a Rootstock user hop through that federation to pay any Lightning payee.

Users only ever touch the layer they are comfortable with. See the full layer list.

{# ====================== DIRECT P2P & ROUTING ======================= #}

Direct peer-to-peer communication

Fast, direct connections with every hop.

Instead of relaying messages blindly down a chain of nodes, Cassis uses Iroh so the payer communicates directly with every node in the route — including the receiver. Payments are fast and simple, with far less room for mysterious routing failures.

Payment speed is only constrained by how long it takes to issue an HTLC on each layer — and on most layers, that is instant.

Direct to every hop via Iroh

The payer coordinates directly with each router and the receiver over encrypted peer-to-peer connections, getting immediate feedback if a hop is unavailable.

Run from home behind NAT

Because Iroh handles NAT traversal out of the box, home router nodes and merchants can operate from home behind NAT with no port forwarding or static IPs.

Optional Tor privacy

Iroh supports Tor, so any peer can remain completely private if they want to — though home routing and direct home receiving work without needing Tor.

Open routing & local pathfinding

Anyone holding funds on two or more networks can run a router and earn fees. Routers announce their pairs on Nostr and payers compute routes locally.
