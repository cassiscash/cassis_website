Layers

The bitcoin layers Cassis routes across, and how each one expresses an HTLC.

Overview

A Cassis payment is a route with any number of hops, each of which is an HTLC, all bound to the same hash H and honoring strictly decreasing timeouts. Each network expresses that HTLC in its own idiom, the preimage R (with H = sha256(R)) is the only key that settles a hop, and a timeout always opens a refund path.

For each layer below:

  • HTLC descriptor is what describes a locked HTLC on the wire: the sender returns it in reply to EXECUTE, and the receiver checks it against the ledger before going on. Every descriptor also carries the target it pays: the claim key (or hold invoice) that can settle it on that network.
  • Default incoming delta is how long a router needs, after learning the preimage at the last possible moment (when its outgoing HTLC expires), to get the claim of its incoming HTLC through on that network before the HTLC can be refunded.
  • Outgoing lag is how long after its expiry an HTLC sent on that network can still be claimed, because the refund path opens by the network's own clock and can lag behind wall time. A router adds this lag to the default incoming delta it announces, so each hop's gap depends on both the network it receives on and the one it sends on (see how it works).
  • Typical cost is what the lock and the claim pay in network or operator fees, in sats, for an ordinary payment. It leaves out the amount locked, Cassis router fees, and one-off on-chain costs such as opening a Lightning channel or boarding a Fedimint; fixed fees dominate small payments and percentage fees scale with the amount (see cost assumptions).

● Lightning

Protocol
BOLT
NetworkId
lightning (mainnet), lightning::signet, lightning::mutinynet, lightning::regtest
HTLC
native BOLT update_add_htlc with payment_hash and cltv_expiry
HTLC descriptor
BOLT11 hold invoice (payment_request), sent as the outgoing target the payer pays verbatim to preserve the payment secret
Default incoming delta
60 min
Outgoing lag
60 min
Typical cost
≈1–2 sats to lock, paid as the Lightning routing fee on the hold invoice (Cassis allows up to max(1 sat, 1%)); nothing to claim offchain. A force-close is a separate on-chain cost.

The hold invoice must be settled before its CLTV runs out, and that is counted in bitcoin blocks, which can come early.

● Liquid

Protocol
Liquid
NetworkId
liquid (mainnet), liquid::testnet
HTLC
P2WSH script, OP_HASH160 <RIPEMD160(payment_hash)> claim path with CLTV refund
HTLC descriptor
lockup outpoint (lockup_txid + lockup_vout) pinning the broadcast lockup tx, refund_pubkey, refund_locktime, and the compressed-secp256k1 target; the claim side is rebuilt from the route hash
Default incoming delta
10 min
Outgoing lag
10 min
Typical cost
≈27 sats to lock at Cassis's 0.1 sat/vB (the relay floor), and ≈40 sats to claim. The sender pays the lock; the claim fee comes out of the HTLC.

Liquid has regular one-minute blocks; the claim must confirm before the refund path opens.

● Arkade

Protocol
Arkade
NetworkId
arkade (arkade.computer, mainnet), arkade::mutinynet (the public mutinynet operator), arkade::<operator host> (each operator is a separate network)
HTLC
VhtlcScript taproot tree, RIPEMD160(payment_hash), seconds-based timelocks
HTLC descriptor
VHTLC options — sender x-only key, refund_locktime, three unilateral CSV delays (unilateral_claim_delay, unilateral_refund_delay, unilateral_refund_without_receiver_delay), and the x-only target (the receiver). The operator is the network's server, and payment_hash160 follows from the route hash
Default incoming delta
20 min
Outgoing lag
60 min
Typical cost
No on-chain fee. The mainnet operator charges 0.2% of each offchain input more than ~29 days from batch expiry (0 when it is nearer), and nothing on offchain outputs, so a lock and claim usually cost about 0.4% of the coins spent between them.

Fees. The mainnet operator charges no transaction fee and nothing on offchain outputs. Its only per-transfer charge is 0.2% of the value of each offchain input whose batch expires more than 29 days away (2,505,216 seconds); inputs closer to expiry cost nothing to spend. An HTLC therefore normally costs 0.2% to lock and 0.2% to claim, but nothing at all when its coins are near the end of their batch. The charge is on the input's value, not the payment: a small HTLC funded from a large VTXO pays 0.2% of the whole coin, so split coins before locking small amounts. An onchain exit output costs 200 sats. Every operator sets its own schedule, published at /v1/info; check the one you use.

Batch expiry. Arkade VTXOs live in batches, and every VTXO expires with the batch it descends from (VTXOs created offchain inherit their inputs' expiry). After that the operator may sweep it, and it can no longer be spent offchain: a VHTLC still running then could neither be claimed nor refunded. So:

  • An HTLC must be over at least 20 minutes before the batch of its VHTLC expires. Receivers reject VHTLCs that are not.
  • Before locking an HTLC, a sender moves coins that would expire too early into a new batch.
  • A router caps how long an incoming HTLC may run with max_htlc_secs in its config (three days by default) and refuses longer ones.
  • Wallet coins older than a batch read as 0 until cassis-wallet arkade --network <id> onboard settles them into a new one.

Dust. Operators refuse outputs below their dust limit (330 sats by default), including change: a payment smaller than that, or one that would leave less change than that, is refused before anything is locked.

● Cashu

Protocol
Cashu
NetworkId
cashu::<host[:port]> (each mint is a separate network)
HTLC
NUT-14 spending conditions on a NUT-10 HTLC secret
HTLC descriptor
NUT-14 locked ecash proofs, one base64-encoded NUT-00 Proof per element, plus the x-only target that can claim them
Default incoming delta
5 min
Outgoing lag
1 min
Typical cost
≈2 sats in total at 100 ppk, paid by the payer: about 1 sat for the lock swap and 1 sat more locked in to cover the claim. Mints with a 0 ppk input fee are free.

Claims are a single request to the mint, timed by the mint's clock. Real mints may charge input fees (e.g. 100 ppk) per proof: the payer locks a little extra to cover the claim's fee, so the payee still nets the invoiced amount.

● Fedimint

Protocol
Fedimint
NetworkId
fedimint::<invite_code> (each fedimint is a separate network)
HTLC
LNv2 direct HTLC: an OutgoingContract funded between two clients of the federation, with no Lightning gateway involved
HTLC descriptor
where the contract was funded (funding_txid + funding_out_idx), the contract itself as consensus JSON, and the x-only target that can claim it
Default incoming delta
40 min
Outgoing lag
60 min
Typical cost
A few sats per side. Consensus fees are 1 sat per input and output, so they grow with the number of e-cash notes touched, plus up to 0.1% per side if the federation sets the maximum relative fee.

Contracts expire after a number of bitcoin blocks, which can come early or late: refunds may only go through a few blocks after the time the HTLC was planned to expire. Consensus fees are per input and output, so a payment that touches several e-cash notes costs a few sats on each side.

● Rootstock

Protocol
Rootstock
NetworkId
rootstock (mainnet), rootstock::testnet
HTLC
EtherSwap v3 contract, sha256 precompile matches H byte-for-byte
HTLC descriptor
amount_wei, refund_address, block-height timelock, and the compressed-secp256k1 target (the claim address)
Default incoming delta
20 min
Outgoing lag
60 min
Typical cost
≈80 sats to lock and ≈140 sats to claim at ~0.03 gwei (about 26k and 46k gas); roughly double at 0.065 gwei and triple at 0.1 gwei. Paid in RBTC gas by the sender and the claimer.

Rootstock has ~30 s blocks with some variance, and its timelocks are block heights. Claiming is a contract call, so the receiver needs a little RBTC for gas, the payee included: without it the claim fails and the whole route stays locked until it expires. Both calls are paid in RBTC, which is pegged 1:1 to BTC, so the gas figures above convert directly to sats.

Cost assumptions

The cost rows are network and operator fees only, in sats, checked on 23 November 2026; they are not quotes. They use Cassis's own settings where it has them (0.1 sat/vB on Liquid, a max(1 sat, 1%) limit on Lightning) and each network's live schedule otherwise. Fixed fees dominate small payments; percentage fees scale with the amount, and mint and operator fees can change without notice. Each hop pays the fees of the network it locks on, so a route's total is the sum over its hops.

The figures come from Blockstream Esplora (Liquid), the Arkade mainnet operator's published fee schedule, sampled Cashu mint keysets (0–100 ppk), Fedimint's LNv2 consensus fee schedule, Rootstock's public RPC and Blockscout gas statistics, and LND's routing defaults. Re-check them before pricing a route.
